# A world worth studying: alien artifacts (AR) and reasons for culture and philosophy

Design (2026-11-27; owner's choice of options 1 and 3, instead of human texts). Nothing here carries human knowledge.
- Every artifact, law and key is drawn by the world's own random generator from a seed.
- Fitness stays the world's: energy and biomass.
- Containment unchanged: the artifacts are cells of the simulated grid, with no input or output beyond it.

Gating (the bootstrap rule):
- AR1 needs LL1 to pass, because agents must be able to learn.
- AR2 needs AR1 and SL1 (social learning).
- AR3 is an analysis of AR2's runs.
- AR4 comes last.

## Why (the owner's question: agents that research and study)

Research is finding hidden rules by experiment and using them. It pays only in a world whose rules are hidden,
discoverable within a life, worth knowing, and not fixed forever.
- **Discoverable but not hard-codable.** If the rules never change, evolution hard-codes the answers. If they cannot
  be discovered, nothing is learned.
- **Precedents in our setting:**
  - Thorndike's puzzle boxes (1898), where the law of effect was found.
  - XLand 2.0's hidden production rules. Agents trained on that open task space showed "hypothesis-driven
    exploration" and adapted to new tasks within minutes (AdA, arXiv:2301.07608).
  - GoalCycle3D's hidden goal orders, where cultural transmission emerged without human data (Bhoopchand et al.,
    Nature Communications 2023).
- **Ours:** the minimal puzzle box, generated by the world.

## AR1: the artifacts (research)

**Artifacts.** A new kind of cell, placed by the world generator at a fixed density. Agents cannot walk onto them.
Each artifact has:
- a visible glyph g in 0..7;
- a hidden key of k tokens from the agents' own symbol alphabet {1, 2, 3}, with k in 1..3 and P(k) proportional to
  2^−k (shorter keys likelier, a simplicity prior);
- a lock with progress 0..k;
- a cooldown.

**Interaction: agents speak to artifacts.** It uses the existing symbol output, which has been unused so far.
- Each tick, an artifact reads the symbol emitted by the first agent (in tick order) that faces it.
- A matching token advances the lock. A wrong non-zero token resets it. Silence leaves it unchanged.
- On completion the artifact pays P energy to the agent that finished it, then rests for C ticks.

**What agents see.** For the artifact in front:
- its glyph;
- its lock progress;
- whether it is resting.

Artifacts in the 5×5 window show their glyph in the window entries. The progress feedback makes keys learnable by
trial and error, like a combination lock with a light, so within-life learning has something to grip.

**Laws and eras.**
- An artifact's key is not its own: key = law[g], one table for the whole world. Knowing the law opens every artifact
  on sight, so knowledge generalises across artifacts.
- The world redraws the law every T_law ticks: a new era.
- Within an era, research pays and its results transfer between artifacts.
- Across eras, facts expire, but the way to find them does not.

**Parameters (to calibrate before the runs):**
- 8 glyphs;
- density 16 per 1024 cells;
- P = 60 energy, C = 100 ticks;
- T_law = 1,000 ticks (twice the maximum lifespan) for AR1.

Calibration target: random speakers open some artifacts, but an agent that knows the law gains at least 3 times as
much energy from artifacts as a random speaker, so knowledge pays.

**World and arms.**
- The W2 world: seasons, view 1, symbols on, so the symbol channel exists and memory already pays.
- The best LL1 arm, with artifacts (arm A) and without (control).

**Measures:**
1. **Artifacts are used:** the champion whose symbols artifacts ignore (`-ablate artmute`) against an intact copy
   (share < 0.5, t < −3).
2. **Research within life:** tokens tried per opening, by agent age. It should fall within each era as agents learn,
   and be below the random-speaker baseline.
3. **Adaptation to a new law:** after an era change, the time to the first openings. Champion against its ancestors,
   and the learning arm against a frozen copy.
4. **Held-out laws:** play in arenas with law tables never drawn in training.

Pass: artifacts used in at least 2 of 3 seeds, and opening efficiency improves with age within eras.
Kill: muting costs nothing in every seed.

## AR2: a reason for culture

Theory:
- Social learning pays when the world changes at an intermediate rate (Boyd and Richerson 1985; reviewed in
  PMC9869458):
  - slower than a lifetime, so what one generation learned is still true for the next;
  - faster than genes can follow, so evolution cannot hard-code it.
- If the world changes too fast, copying spreads outdated information. If too slow, genes take over.
- Pure copying adds nothing on its own (Rogers' paradox, 1988). Copying then checking by one's own learning resolves
  it (critical social learning; Enquist, Eriksson and Ghirlanda 2007, Am. Anthropol. 109:727).
- SL1 is critical by construction: taught synapses plus each agent's own learning.

Design:
- Era length T_law = 0.2, 1, 5 and 25 times the maximum lifespan (500 ticks).
- Arms: learning only (best LL1 arm) and learning with social learning (best SL1 arm).
- 3 seeds each.

Prediction, fixed before the runs: the value of transmission (the cost of `-ablate noteach` or `noobs` to the
champion) is highest at intermediate eras (1–5 lifespans), and small at 0.2 (too fast) and at 25 (slow enough for
genes).
- A hump is a measured confirmation of cultural evolution theory inside our world.
- A flat profile means transmission is not doing what culture does.

Kill: transmission costs nothing at any era length.

## AR3: a reason for philosophy (knowledge that outlives its facts)

Operational definition: transmitted knowledge that keeps its value after the particular facts it came with have
expired. Specific keys and laws die with their era. Methods do not:
- using the progress light;
- not repeating a failed token;
- inferring a glyph's key from similar glyphs;
- when to trust a teacher and when to test.

The reason it should emerge:
- Laws are not arbitrary tables. Each era's law is drawn from a hidden family, for example a shift or reversal of
  the token order under a glyph permutation. There is a "law of the laws".
- Knowledge then sits at three levels, each lasting longer than the one below:

| Level | Example | Lasts | Natural carrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | this glyph's key | one era | individual learning |
| Laws | this era's glyph-to-key table | one era, all artifacts | culture |
| Law of laws | which family laws come from | forever | method passed on, or genes |

- Lineages that pass on methods recover faster after every change than lineages that pass on facts. Selection
  between lineages favours abstract knowledge.

Measure, from AR2's runs:
- Taught newborns' advantage within their teacher's era (facts and methods) against their advantage in a new era
  that the teacher never saw (methods only).
- Philosophy, in this narrow sense, exists if the second is above zero: teaching still helps when everything taught
  as fact is obsolete.
- Control: the same runs with eras drawn from unrelated random tables (no law of laws). There the new-era advantage
  should vanish.

## AR4: a reason for society and writing (later)

- **Joint artifacts.** Locks that open only when two agents facing them emit complementary tokens in the same tick.
  This gives conventions a reason (Lewis 1969; Skyrms 2010): shared symbols and who says what. Together with
  giving (IN1), it raises the questions norms answer: who pays, who shares.
- **Inscriptions.** Agents mark an artifact with a token that later agents see: knowledge stored outside any living
  agent.
  - Test: after a catastrophe that kills most of a lineage, lineages with inscriptions recover faster.
  - Control: wiping the inscriptions (a Dark Ages test).

## Implementation plan (AR1)

- `WParams`:
  - `art` (artifacts per 1024 cells);
  - `art_glyphs` 8, `art_pay` 60, `art_rest` 100;
  - `art_era` (T_law ticks);
  - `art_family` (0 random tables; 1 a hidden family, for AR3).
- `World`, per cell, saved:
  - artifact glyph (−1 none), lock progress, rest timer;
  - the current law table, drawn from the seed and the era index (deterministic).
- Interaction in `tick` after symbols are drawn:
  - an artifact reads the facing agent's symbol;
  - the payoff goes to that agent's energy;
  - counters for analysis: openings, tokens tried, by lineage.
- Observation, signals worlds (din 128) without matter: entries 79–103 show artifacts in the window as 16·(glyph + 1);
  entry 104 the progress of the artifact in front (32 per token); 105 its rest state (−64 resting).
- Ablation `artmute`: artifacts ignore A's symbols.
- Tests: key law and era redraw are deterministic; a correct sequence opens and pays; a wrong token resets;
  bit-exact resume.
