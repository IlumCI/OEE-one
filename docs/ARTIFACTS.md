# A world worth studying: alien artifacts (AR) and reasons for culture and philosophy

Design (2026-11-27; owner's choice of options 1 and 3, instead of human texts). Nothing here carries human knowledge.
- Every artifact, law and key is drawn by the world's own random generator from a seed.
- Fitness stays the world's: energy and biomass.
- Containment unchanged: the artifacts are cells of the simulated grid, with no input or output beyond it.

Gating (the bootstrap rule):
- AR1 needs LL1 to pass, because agents must be able to learn. Since 2026-11-27 the gate is LL1c (LIFE.md): AR1
  rewards pooled knowledge rather than individual learning (value of information, below).
- AR2 needs AR1 and SL1 (social learning).
- AR3 is an analysis of AR2's runs.
- AR4 comes last.

## Why (the owner's question: agents that research and study)

Research is finding hidden rules by experiment and using them. It pays only in a world whose rules are hidden,
discoverable within a life, worth knowing, and not fixed forever.
- **Discoverable but not hard-codable.** If the rules never change, evolution hard-codes the answers. If they cannot
  be discovered, nothing is learned.
- **Precedents in our setting:**
  - Thorndike's puzzle boxes (1898), where the law of effect was found.
  - XLand 2.0's hidden production rules. Agents trained on that open task space showed "hypothesis-driven
    exploration" and adapted to new tasks within minutes (AdA, arXiv:2301.07608).
  - GoalCycle3D's hidden goal orders, where cultural transmission emerged without human data (Bhoopchand et al.,
    Nature Communications 2023).
- **Ours:** the minimal puzzle box, generated by the world.

## AR1: the artifacts (research)

**Artifacts.** A new kind of cell, placed by the world generator at a fixed density. Agents cannot walk onto them.
Each artifact has:
- a visible glyph g in 0..7;
- a hidden key of k tokens from the agents' own symbol alphabet {1, 2, 3}, with k in 1..3 and P(k) proportional to
  2^−k (shorter keys likelier, a simplicity prior);
- a lock with progress 0..k;
- a cooldown.

**Interaction: agents speak to artifacts.** It uses the existing symbol output, which has been unused so far.
- Each tick, an artifact reads the symbol emitted by the first agent (in tick order) that faces it.
- A matching token advances the lock. A wrong non-zero token resets it. Silence leaves it unchanged.
- On completion the artifact pays P energy to the agent that finished it, then rests for C ticks.

**What agents see.** For the artifact in front:
- its glyph;
- its lock progress;
- whether it is resting.

Artifacts in the 5×5 window show their glyph in the window entries. The progress feedback makes keys learnable by
trial and error, like a combination lock with a light, so within-life learning has something to grip.

**Laws and eras.**
- An artifact's key is not its own: key = law[g], one table for the whole world. Knowing the law opens every artifact
  on sight, so knowledge generalises across artifacts.
- The world redraws the law every T_law ticks: a new era.
- Within an era, research pays and its results transfer between artifacts.
- Across eras, facts expire, but the way to find them does not.

**Parameters (calibrated 2026-11-27, before the runs):**
- 8 glyphs;
- density 32 per 1024 cells;
- P = 300 energy, C = 100 ticks;
- a wrong token also locks the artifact for 30 ticks (`-art_lock 30`), so guessing is costly;
- T_law = 1,000 ticks (twice the maximum lifespan) for AR1.

Calibration target: an agent that knows the law gains at least 3 times as much energy from artifacts as a random
speaker, and knowing it pays in fitness.

Calibration:
- Genome: the W2 champion (runs8/w2_sig_s1, epoch 3000), which evolved without artifacts and speaks at random.
- Method: the oracle analysis (`-ablate artoracle`: A speaks the right token to any ready artifact it faces), 8 seeds
  × 2.

| 16 | 60 | 0 | 0.76 / 2.03 | 46 / 122 | 0.50 (t 0.0) |

- Without a lockout, chance openings by random speaking are too common, and the payoff too small for knowledge to
  matter (oracle share 0.50).
- With the chosen settings:
  - knowing the law opens 3.8 times as many artifacts;
  - artifacts supply about 40% of a knowing agent's food;
  - the oracle copy wins (0.87).
- So knowledge pays, and the research problem is real.

**Value of information (measured 2026-11-27, before any AR1 run; `-art_code 1`; 16 seeds × 2 unless noted).**
The W2 champion is played against itself, with A replaced by an analysis policy whenever it faces a ready artifact:
- **guess:** always speak a random token.
- **own memory:** a perfect individual learner. It speaks the token its own memory knows for the lock state (glyph,
  progress), otherwise an untried one, and forgets a known token when it fails.
- **lineage memory:** the same, with one memory shared by the whole lineage. This is perfect pooled (cultural)
  knowledge.
- **oracle:** knows the law.

| Glyphs | Openings/kt: intact / guess / own memory / lineage memory / oracle | Share of A: guess / own / lineage / oracle |
|---|---|---|
| 8 (8 seeds) | 1.02 / 1.33 / 1.65 / 3.65 / 3.79 | 0.56 / 0.69 / 0.69 / 0.75 |
| 4 | – / 1.48 / 1.88 / 3.82 / 3.91 | 0.50 / 0.59 / 0.78 / 0.81 |
| 2 | – / 1.37 / 2.11 / 3.87 / 3.94 | 0.66 / 0.60 / 0.84 / 0.81 |
| 1 | 0.88 / 1.34 / 2.48 / 3.90 / 3.95 | 0.56 / 0.59 / 0.75 / 0.78 |

- An agent speaks to a ready artifact only about 4 times per life (5–8 tokens per thousand agent-ticks).
- Even with one glyph, a perfect individual learner needs 200–300 ticks of its life to bring its tokens per opening
  down from about 6 to about 2.
- So a perfect individual learner recovers about a quarter to a third of the value of knowing the law. A perfect
  lineage memory recovers about 95%.
- Consequence: AR1 does not reward individual learning ("learning not to learn": learning pays only if it pays off
  within a lifetime; Lange and Sprekeler, arXiv:2010.04466). It rewards pooled knowledge.
  - AR1 is therefore a world for culture (AR2), entered after individual learning has passed where it can pay.
  - That test is LL1c (LIFE.md).

**Learner fix (2026-11-27).** The LL1 plastic layer covers the action logits only, and symbols come from logits 8–11.
As built, learning could not change what an agent says to an artifact. Fixes, off by default:
- `-life_sym 1`: a plastic symbol head (its own softmax, the same reward). `-life_seta` sets its learning rate;
  `-life_symh 0` makes it read only the bias and the raw inputs.
- `-life_in A,B`: the plastic synapses also read raw observation entries A to B−1.
- `-art_code 1`: the artifact in front is shown as one of 24 lines (glyph × progress). This is a categorical lock state
  that a linear plastic readout can map to a token.

With the W2 champion and learning on, tokens per opening did not change (frozen 4.9, learning 4.6). That is expected
from the experience rate above.

**World and arms.**
- The W2 world: seasons, view 1, symbols on, so the symbol channel exists and memory already pays.
- The best LL1 arm, with artifacts (arm A) and without (control).

**Measures:**
1. **Artifacts are used:** the champion whose symbols artifacts ignore (`-ablate artmute`) against an intact copy
   (share < 0.5, t < −3).
2. **Research within life:** tokens tried per opening, by agent age. It should fall within each era as agents learn,
   and be below the random-speaker baseline.
3. **Adaptation to a new law:** after an era change, the time to the first openings. Champion against its ancestors,
   and the learning arm against a frozen copy.
4. **Held-out laws:** play in arenas with law tables never drawn in training.

Pass: artifacts used in at least 2 of 3 seeds, and opening efficiency improves with age within eras.
Kill: muting costs nothing in every seed.

## AR2: a reason for culture

Theory:
- Social learning pays when the world changes at an intermediate rate (Boyd and Richerson 1985; reviewed in
  PMC9869458):
  - slower than a lifetime, so what one generation learned is still true for the next;
  - faster than genes can follow, so evolution cannot hard-code it.
- If the world changes too fast, copying spreads outdated information. If too slow, genes take over.
- Pure copying adds nothing on its own (Rogers' paradox, 1988). Copying then checking by one's own learning resolves
  it (critical social learning; Enquist, Eriksson and Ghirlanda 2007, Am. Anthropol. 109:727).
- SL1 is critical by construction: taught synapses plus each agent's own learning.

Design:
- Era length T_law = 0.2, 1, 5 and 25 times the maximum lifespan (500 ticks).
- Arms: learning only (best LL1 arm) and learning with social learning (best SL1 arm).
- 3 seeds each.

Prediction, fixed before the runs: the value of transmission (the cost of `-ablate noteach` or `noobs` to the
champion) is highest at intermediate eras (1–5 lifespans), and small at 0.2 (too fast) and at 25 (slow enough for
genes).
- A hump is a measured confirmation of cultural evolution theory inside our world.
- A flat profile means transmission is not doing what culture does.

Kill: transmission costs nothing at any era length.

## AR3: a reason for philosophy (knowledge that outlives its facts)

Operational definition: transmitted knowledge that keeps its value after the particular facts it came with have
expired. Specific keys and laws die with their era. Methods do not:
- using the progress light;
- not repeating a failed token;
- inferring a glyph's key from similar glyphs;
- when to trust a teacher and when to test.

The reason it should emerge:
- Laws are not arbitrary tables. Each era's law is drawn from a hidden family, for example a shift or reversal of
  the token order under a glyph permutation. There is a "law of the laws".
- Knowledge then sits at three levels, each lasting longer than the one below:

| Level | Example | Lasts | Natural carrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | this glyph's key | one era | individual learning |
| Laws | this era's glyph-to-key table | one era, all artifacts | culture |
| Law of laws | which family laws come from | forever | method passed on, or genes |

- Lineages that pass on methods recover faster after every change than lineages that pass on facts. Selection
  between lineages favours abstract knowledge.

Measure, from AR2's runs:
- Taught newborns' advantage within their teacher's era (facts and methods) against their advantage in a new era
  that the teacher never saw (methods only).
- Philosophy, in this narrow sense, exists if the second is above zero: teaching still helps when everything taught
  as fact is obsolete.
- Control: the same runs with eras drawn from unrelated random tables (no law of laws). There the new-era advantage
  should vanish.

## AR4: a reason for society and writing (later)

- **Joint artifacts.** Locks that open only when two agents facing them emit complementary tokens in the same tick.
  This gives conventions a reason (Lewis 1969; Skyrms 2010): shared symbols and who says what. Together with
  giving (IN1), it raises the questions norms answer: who pays, who shares.
- **Inscriptions.** Agents mark an artifact with a token that later agents see: knowledge stored outside any living
  agent.
  - Test: after a catastrophe that kills most of a lineage, lineages with inscriptions recover faster.
  - Control: wiping the inscriptions (a Dark Ages test).

## Implementation plan (AR1)

- `WParams` (built 2026-11-27, with `-art_lock` and the analysis `-ablate artoracle`):
  - `art` (artifacts per 1024 cells);
  - `art_glyphs` 8, `art_pay` 60, `art_rest` 100;
  - `art_era` (T_law ticks);
  - `art_family` (0 random tables; 1 a hidden family, for AR3).
- `World`, per cell, saved:
  - artifact glyph (−1 none), lock progress, rest timer;
  - the current law table, drawn from the seed and the era index (deterministic).
- Interaction in `tick` after symbols are drawn:
  - an artifact reads the facing agent's symbol;
  - the payoff goes to that agent's energy;
  - counters for analysis: openings, tokens tried, by lineage.
- Observation, signals worlds (din 128) without matter: entries 79–103 show artifacts in the window as 16·(glyph + 1);
  entry 104 the progress of the artifact in front (32 per token); 105 its rest state (−64 resting).
- Ablation `artmute`: artifacts ignore A's symbols.
- Tests: key law and era redraw are deterministic; a correct sequence opens and pays; a wrong token resets;
  bit-exact resume.

## Culture-world calibration (protocol fixed 2026-11-28 05:17 UTC, before the runs)

Chosen when the owner's decision timer expired with the recommended option applied, after the SL1 V verdict
(SOCIAL.md).
- The limit there is the food world's knowledge ceiling: there is little to learn, and breeding substitutes for it.
- The artifacts world has a large ceiling. Pooled knowledge is worth about 3 times individual knowledge (value of
  information, above), and an artifact cannot be opened without knowing its key.
- No long runs before the owner sees this calibration.

World: the W2 world of runs8/w2_sig_s1 with `-art 32 -art_pay 300 -art_rest 100 -art_lock 30 -art_era 1000
-art_glyphs 8 -art_code 1`.

Learner (LT): the LL1c learner on the symbol head with teaching:
`-life 1 -life_eta 12 -life_lam 230 -life_beta 7 -life_sym 1 -life_in 104,128 -life_xeta 7 -life_teach 1`.

(a) **Viability from random founders over 1,000 epochs** (the LL1c lesson: 250 was too short).
- Arms E (no learning) and LT, seeds 1–3: runs8/w2ar_{E,LT}_s{1,2,3}, binary runs8/oee_w_v29.
- Viable: no more than 10 reseeds in any 200 epochs.

(b) **Teaching accumulates the law** (fixed genome: the W2 champion runs8/w2_sig_s1 epoch 3000).
- Eras of 4,000 ticks and games of 8,000 ticks, so many generations see each law.
- Comparisons, 16 seeds × 2:
  - A learning with teaching against B learning without (`-teach_b 0`);
  - A frozen against B learning with teaching.
- Measures: openings per thousand agent-ticks, tokens per opening, and tokens per opening of newborns (ages 0–99).
- Accumulation shows if teaching lowers newborns' tokens per opening below the non-teaching learner's.

(c) The value of information with `-art_code 1` is already measured (above): oracle share 0.75–0.81, individual
memory 0.59–0.75, lineage memory 0.69–0.84.

Decision after the calibration, by the owner: the culture runs (E, LT, LT plus observation; 6 seeds) or a change of
world.

### Calibration (b) result (2026-11-28 05:23 UTC): teaching does not accumulate the law

| Comparison | Tokens per opening, A / B | Newborn tokens per opening, A / B | Openings per thousand agent-ticks, A / B | Share of A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A teaching (1/2) against B learning only | 5.2 / 5.4 | 5.29 / 5.59 | 1.05 / 1.00 | 0.34 (t −2.1) |
| A frozen against B teaching (1/2) | 5.7 / 5.1 | 5.62 / 5.30 | 0.94 / 1.06 | 0.59 (t 1.9) |
| A without teaching against B teaching that keeps 7/8 (`-life_tkeep 3`) | 5.3 / 5.0 | 5.34 / 5.13 | 1.03 / 1.09 | 0.56 (t 1.0) |
| A without observation against B with observation (`-life_obs 1`) | 5.3 / 5.4 | 5.38 / 5.64 | 1.01 / 0.97 | 0.47 (t −0.6) |

- Random speaking gives about 5–6 tokens per opening, and the oracle about 1.5.
- No learner variant moves far from random: not teaching at either fidelity, not observation.
- Halving at birth caps what a lineage carries at about twice one life's learning. But even at 7/8 there is
  nothing to accumulate. An individual speaks to a ready artifact only about 3 times per life, so individual
  learning yields almost nothing to pass on.
- So the culture world cannot start from this genome. It would need evolution to first raise how often agents
  engage artifacts, then learning, then transmission. That is three capabilities in sequence, which the bootstrap
  rule warns against.
- Whether evolution raises engagement is visible in calibration (a): openings by the E and LT champions at epoch
  1,000.
- Added for this check: `-life_tkeep k` (teaching keeps 1 − 2^−k of the parent's synapses; default 1, half,
  unchanged).

### Calibration (a), interim (2026-11-28 06:20 UTC)

- **E is viable from random founders.** Seeds 1 and 2 had 0 reseeds in 1,000 epochs.
- **Seed 3 and the LT runs are under way,** also with 0 reseeds so far. LT is much slower per epoch, with larger
  populations.
- **Evolution raises engagement only modestly.** E seed 1 at epoch 1,000 against itself (16 seeds × 2), compared
  with the W2 champion:
  - it speaks on 0.76 of ticks against 0.26;
  - tokens to ready artifacts per thousand agent-ticks: 6.6 against 5.1;
  - openings: 1.17 against 0.90;
  - tokens per opening: 5.6 against 5.7, so no knowledge, as expected without learning;
  - it beats the W2 champion (0.65, t 2.6).
- So 1,000 epochs bring about 30% more artifact use. An agent still speaks to a ready artifact about 3–4 times per
  life, which is too few to learn the law.
- **E seed 3 is viable too** (0 reseeds in 1,000 epochs). All three E seeds pass.
- **LT seed 1 at epoch 500 does not use what it has.** Against itself it gets 5.2 tokens per opening (E seed 1 at
  epoch 500: 5.8) and 1.23 openings per thousand agent-ticks (E: 1.18). But `-ablate freeze` costs nothing (0.49,
  t −0.15), and neither does `-ablate noteach` (0.50, t −0.02).
- In the food-kinds world, freezing was fatal by epoch 2,000. Here, after 500 epochs, the lineage neither depends on
  learning nor on teaching: there is too little to learn (rule 3 below). The same test is repeated at epoch 1,000.
- **At epoch 1,000 (LT seed 1; docs/results/ar_cal/w2ar_LT_s1.txt) the picture is the same:**

**Calibration verdict (2026-11-28 08:45 UTC).**
- The artifacts world is viable: E 0, 0, 0 reseeds in 1,000 epochs; LT 0 so far.
- But it does not exercise knowledge enough for learning or culture to start. No learner variant moved tokens per
  opening off the random level, with a fixed genome or after 1,000 epochs of evolution, and learning and teaching
  are not used.
- Culture runs in this world would be expected to fail for the measured reason (rule 3). So the decision goes back
  to the owner: AR5 (locked food), or another direction.


## A design rule from LL1c, SL1 V and this calibration (2026-11-28)

Knowledge drives the evolution of learning and culture only if it is:
1. **valuable:** knowing it raises fitness a lot;
2. **non-substitutable:** no other evolvable strategy gets the same fitness without it;
3. **exercised:** many trials per life, so it can be learned within a life and then passed on.

Applied to the worlds tested:
- **The food-kinds world** has 1 and 3 (about 200 eats per life), not 2. E lineages match learners by breeding fast
  (SOCIAL.md, SL1 V verdict).
- **The artifacts world** has 1 and largely 2: an artifact cannot be opened without its key. It lacks 3: about 3
  trials per life.

## Proposal AR5: locked food (not built; for the owner's decision)

- Food comes only from artifacts: no free food, or little. An opened artifact releases food. The law (glyph to key)
  is redrawn each era.
- Every meal becomes a trial, so 3 holds. Fitness cannot be had without the key, so 2 holds.
- The world must stay viable for naive agents, so guessing must still feed a population. Short keys and a short
  lockout are the levers, and they trade viability against the value of knowing.
- That trade-off is calibrated first: viability from random founders over at least 1,000 epochs, and the value of
  information.
- Related benchmarks with hidden rules redrawn per episode: Alchemy (Wang et al. 2021, arXiv:2102.02926), where
  meta-learning agents failed; XLand-MiniGrid (Nikulin et al., arXiv:2312.12044), hidden production rules.
