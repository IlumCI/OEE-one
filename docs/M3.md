# M3: Chemistry Layer (L1). BFF Primordial Soup

The question for this layer: does self-replication arise on its own from random code?
Geb has reproduction built into its rules. BFF has nothing of the kind.

Reference: Agüera y Arcas et al., "Computational Life: How Well-formed, Self-replicating
Programs Emerge from Simple Interaction", arXiv:2406.19108 (v2, 2024).

## Implementation
`src/bff`, `apps/oee_bff`. This is an independent C++ reimplementation of the reference
code cubff (Apache-2.0; github.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff, language
`bff_noheads`).

- **Interpreter.** Ten instructions on one 128-byte tape:
  - `<` `>` move head 0, and `{` `}` move head 1;
  - `+` `-` change the byte under head 0;
  - `.` copies the byte under head 0 to head 1, and `,` copies the other way;
  - `[` `]` loop while the byte under head 0 is non-zero.

  Both heads and the instruction pointer start at 0, and heads wrap modulo 128. A run
  ends after 8192 steps, when the instruction pointer leaves the tape, or at an
  unmatched bracket.
- **Soup.** 131,072 programs of 64 random bytes. In each epoch, a random permutation
  pairs the programs. Each pair is concatenated into one tape, mutated, executed and
  split back into two programs. There is no fitness function and no copying built in.
- **Mutation.** Each byte has a 2^-12 (0.024%) chance per interaction of being replaced
  by a random byte.
- **Measure.** High-order entropy is the soup's byte entropy minus its brotli bits per
  byte (quality 2, window 24). The paper calls it a transition when this reaches 1.
- **Self-replicator check (cubff `CheckSelfRep`).** The program is paired with 13 noise
  tapes and run for 5 generations. Positions that are reproduced consistently are
  counted; 5 or more on both halves marks it as a self-replicator.

**Validation (ctest)**
- `bff_cubff_equivalence`: reproduces cubff's shipped 256-epoch reference trajectory
  byte for byte, using seed 10248 and the same brotli version (1.1.0).
- `bff_resume`: a run resumed from a checkpoint is identical to an uninterrupted run.
- `bff_selfrep_controls`: a hand-written palindromic copier scores 64/64 and copies
  itself exactly in one interaction. A random program scores 0.

### Results
1. **Transition rate reproduced.**
   - 9 of 20 seeds (45%; 95% Wilson CI 26-66%) reach high-order entropy ≥ 1 within
     16,384 epochs.
   - The paper reports about 40% of 1000 runs.
   - Transition epochs range from 2305 to 14,081, consistent with the paper's
     observation that a transition can occur at any point.
2. **Emergence is not always stable.** 3 of the 9 transitions collapsed within 2048
   epochs (seeds 8, 11, 16).
   - Entropy rose to 1.3-3.2, then fell back to the random baseline.
   - Self-replicators were extinct or nearly so at the end: 0.1%, 0.0% and 0.0%.
   - The paper describes "zero-poisoning" episodes after a first replicator appears.
     Here, in 3 of 9 cases, the soup never recovered within the window.
3. **Independent origins, different replicators.** The dominant program differs between
   seeds (no-op bytes shown as `_`):
   ```
   seed 0   __[[<,__________}__]___}_________,________.__.__________,<[_.___
   seed 2   _____[_______<__,__}_________]]_________}__,__<_______[_________
   seed 7   [_______<_____,}________________________________},_____<________
   seed 10  _____________{___[__.___{>___________]]___________>{___.___[__{_
   seed 12  ____________[_{.>______<___>__]_>___<______>.{_[________________
   seed 15  _[[__[_[[[[[[_{__._____,>]____[+_[__[_____[_[__]>,_+___.__{_[_[[
   ```
   - Seeds 0, 2 and 7 copy with `,` (pull: tape[head0] = tape[head1]).
   - Seeds 10 and 12 copy with `.` (push), together with head-1 moves `{`.
   - Seed 15 has a bracket-heavy loop structure.
   - Execution cost at the end differs by an order of magnitude: from 268 instructions
     per interaction (seed 7) to 2268 (seed 2).

   Each origin found its own solution to self-copying.
4. **Seed 7 caveat.** The soup collapsed to 2387 distinct programs with entropy 4.39,
   but only 20.5% pass the replicator check. The likely cause is that the dominant
   family copies itself only in some pairings or orientations, which the fixed-noise
   check under-counts. This is not verified.

## MSPD: soups with replicators against soups without
`tools/mspd/bff_mspd.py`, the same estimator as Geb in [M2.md](M2.md).
- **Data.** Ten final soups were continued for 575 epochs. The first 4096 programs were
  dumped every epoch.
- **Carriers.** Byte positions, which persist across epochs even though partners change
  every epoch.
- **State.** The byte's instruction class: 13 classes (zero, two no-op shades, ten
  commands).
- **Parameters.** Window 32 epochs, step 8, lags 1 and 4.

| soup | runs | MSPD | mean heterogeneity h |
|---|---|---|---|
| replicators held (seeds 0, 2, 12, 15) | 4 | 0.0024-0.0054 | 0.07-0.15 |
| no transition (seeds 1, 3, 4, 5) | 4 | 0.0013-0.0018 | 0.35 |
| collapsed (seeds 11, 16) | 2 | 0.0014 | 0.36-0.37 |

- **Separation.** Every soup with established replicators scores above every soup
  without them. One-sided exact Mann-Whitney test, 4 vs 6 runs: p = 0.005, the minimum
  attainable.
- **Same signature as Geb.**
  - Random soups have high stationary heterogeneity (h ≈ 0.35): every byte position
    behaves differently, but the pattern does not change over time.
  - Replicator soups have less heterogeneity (h ≈ 0.07-0.15), but it is reorganised over
    time, as families compete and copies overwrite one another.
  - MSPD therefore separates evolving from non-evolving dynamics in two unrelated
    substrates: spatial agents (L2) and a well-mixed program soup (L1).
- **Effect size.** About 2-4 times, smaller than Geb's 20-30 times.
- **Collapsed soups** are indistinguishable from soups that never transitioned. This is
  consistent with their reversion to random code.
