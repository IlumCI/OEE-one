# Reproducing Two Routes to Open-Ended Evolution, with Calibrated Controls: Geb, BFF, and a First Attempt to Couple Them

*Draft, 11 November 2026. Author: ilumCI (OEE-one). All numbers are taken from files
in this repository; sections marked PENDING depend on runs still in progress.*

## Abstract
We reimplement two artificial-life systems that report open-ended evolutionary
dynamics: Channon's Geb and the BFF primordial soup of Agüera y Arcas et al. Both are
written in C++20 and match their reference implementations byte for byte on reference
data.

We then test their headline claims under stricter measurement.

**Geb**
- **Null control.** Each run carries an independent second shadow population, which
  serves as a matched null.
- **Activity.** In five seeds, component-normalised evolutionary activity grows over
  2-8 million steps, while every null stays near zero.
- **Calibrated test.** Real-run slopes exceed every null window. A paired permutation
  test reaches the minimum p-value attainable with five seeds (p = 0.031; Hedges'
  g = 1.7-4.5).
- **Scaling.** Channon's 2019 result reproduces in shape. Maximum individual complexity
  rises with joint scaling of world size and neuron limit (9.19 + 2.66 log2(L/10);
  R² = 0.92) and saturates when only the neuron limit grows.
- **Stagnation.** Populations sometimes stop reproducing. A rollback-and-reseed
  procedure recovers these runs without changing Geb's rules.

**BFF**
- **Emergence.** Self-replicators arise from random code in 9 of 20 runs within 16,384
  epochs (45%; 95% CI 26-66%), consistent with the reported ~40%.
- **Instability.** In 3 of the 9 runs, the replicators went extinct again within 2048
  epochs.
- **Diversity.** Independent origins produce structurally different replicators.
- **Spatial variant.** In the 2D variant, replicators arise at a comparable interaction
  budget but remain spatially localised.

**MSPD**
We adapt Multi-Scale Path Divergence (MSPD), a 2026 metric of temporally organised
heterogeneity, to discrete substrates.
- **Separation.** It separates evolved from unevolved Geb worlds (20-30 times) and
  replicator soups from random soups (2-4 times).
- **Limits.** It fails in patchy 2D soups. It does not track growth within an evolving
  run.
- **Implementation note.** Two choices left open in the original formulation must be
  fixed for discrete data, and shuffled surrogates are not a valid null for this
  functional.

**Coupling (preliminary)**
Finally, we report a first attempt to couple the two systems. The genome of a Geb agent
is executed as BFF code to produce its offspring, and the founders are replicators that
evolved in BFF soups. With these founders, heredity falls to a low floor of 6-8 of 64
bytes. Geb's unbounded activity is lost (class 1 versus 3b), a manifestation of an
error threshold.

## 1. Introduction
Open-ended evolution (OEE) is the continual production of novel adaptive forms. It is a
central goal of artificial life, and claims of it are notoriously hard to test.

**The two systems studied here**

| System | Level at which the claim is made | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Geb (Channon 1998-2019) | Ecology of embodied agents | Agents with developmentally grown neural networks reproduce and kill through behaviour, with no explicit fitness function. Channon reports class-3 (unbounded) evolutionary activity under a component-normalised Bedau-Packard test (Channon 2006), and that maximum individual complexity scales without bound when world size and neuron limit grow together (Channon 2019). |
| BFF (Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024) | Origin of replication | In a soup of random self-modifying programs with no fitness function, self-replicators arise spontaneously in a large fraction of runs. |

Both results are influential. Neither has, to our knowledge, been independently
reimplemented with matched null controls. This paper does that, and asks three
questions:
1. Do the published results hold in exact reimplementations?
2. Do they survive calibrated statistics against nulls generated by the same code?
3. Does a single, substrate-agnostic dynamics metric distinguish evolving from
   non-evolving dynamics in both?

We then take a first step toward combining the systems: replicators that emerge in BFF
become the heredity machinery of Geb agents.

**Contributions**
1. Bit-exact C++20 reimplementations of Geb v08b3 and BFF (cubff `bff_noheads`), with
   checkpoint/resume and equivalence tests.
2. An independent second-shadow null for Geb's normalised activity statistics, and a
   test calibrated on that null.
3. A reproduction of Channon's 2019 scaling result at reduced scale.
4. A rollback-and-reseed procedure for stagnated Geb runs.
5. A 20-seed BFF reproduction, with an analysis of replicator instability, replicator
   diversity, and the 2D variant.
6. An adaptation of MSPD to discrete substrates, with its failure cases.
7. A negative result on coupling: replicator-performed heredity falls below the level
   needed to sustain Geb's unbounded activity.

## 2. Related work
**Evolutionary activity and classification.** Bedau and Packard's activity statistics,
the class 1/2/3 taxonomy, and Channon's component-normalised variant with a reset
shadow population (Channon 2006).

**Geb.** Channon and Damper 1998; Channon 2001, 2003, 2006, 2019.

**Self-replication from random code.** Tierra (Ray 1991), Coreworld (Rasmussen et al.
1990), Amoeba (Pargellis 1996, 2001), Nanopond, and BFF (Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024).
Knierim et al. (2026) show that BFF replicators are also reachable by random mutation
walks.

**Heredity performed by code in embodied or spatial agents.** Avida, including its
spatial and predation variants (Ofria and Wilke 2004; Grabowski et al. 2008, 2013;
Wagner et al. 2020); SwarmEvolve 2.0 (Spector et al. 2003, 2005); Cosmos (Taylor 1999).

**Function and replication in soups.** Cicala et al. 2026 (arXiv 2607.09211); Jha et al.
2026 (arXiv 2609.10817).

**Dynamics metrics.** MSPD (Akhtyrchenko et al. 2026, arXiv 2606.17091). Foundation-model
search for artificial life (ASAL, Kumar et al. 2024, arXiv 2412.17799).

## 3. Systems and reimplementation
### 3.1 Geb
- **Source.** Geb v08b3 (Channon, MIT licence) was ported to C++20 (`src/geb`). The
  specification, including the quirks the port reproduces, is in `docs/GEB_SPEC.md`.
- **Equivalence.** Six golden cases of 5000 steps each, generated by the original binary,
  are reproduced byte for byte (`golden_equivalence`).
- **Resume.** Runs resumed from checkpoints are byte-identical to uninterrupted runs
  (`checkpoint_resume`).
- **Performance.** Bit-parallel rule decoding, among other changes, makes runs 3.5-4
  times faster with identical output. This was verified by fuzzing against the reference
  decoder and by A/B tests from late-stage checkpoints.

### 3.2 BFF
- **Source.** An independent reimplementation of cubff `bff_noheads` (Apache-2.0), in
  `src/bff`.
- **Matching details.** The random streams (SplitMix64 keyed by seed, epoch and index),
  the interpreter (heads and instruction pointer starting at 0, heads wrapping modulo
  128, 8192-step limit), pairing by random permutation, the mutation rate (2^-12 per
  byte per interaction), and the high-order-entropy metric (brotli quality 2, window 24,
  library 1.1.0) all match the reference.
- **Equivalence.** cubff's shipped 256-epoch reference trajectory for seed 10248 is
  reproduced byte for byte (`bff_cubff_equivalence`).
- **Replicator check.** Ported from cubff and validated with a positive control (a
  hand-written copier scores 64/64) and a negative control (random code scores 0).
- **2D variant.** Implemented following cubff's `allowed_interactions` pairing. It has
  no reference trajectory to compare against.

## 4. Measurement
### 4.1 Normalised activity with an independent null
- **Components** are production-rule predecessors plus link bits (Channon 2006).
- **Normalisation** uses Channon's reset shadow.
- **Null.** We add a second reset shadow driven by its own random stream and analyse it
  exactly as if it were the real run.
- **Stagnated runs** are truncated at their last birth.

### 4.2 Calibrated test
**Statistic.** The Theil-Sen slope of normalised median activity over the second half
of a horizon [H/2, H].

**Per-run test.** The reference distribution is the set of slopes from every null
trajectory over every window of the same length. A null is never judged against its own
windows (leave-one-out).

**Group test.** An exact paired sign-flip test over seeds, with Hedges' g as the effect
size.

### 4.3 MSPD for discrete substrates
We follow Akhtyrchenko et al. (2026), Eqs. 10-17.

**Construction**
- **Carriers.** Discrete carriers: world cells in Geb, byte positions in BFF.
- **Window law.** The window law of transition pairs.
- **Heterogeneity.** Jensen-Shannon heterogeneity, with a pooled-transition null
  subtracted.
- **Scale functional.** A dyadic block-averaging functional (Eq. 15).

**Choices not fixed by the original formulation**
1. **Significance floor.** φ(x) = max(x − 2·sd(H0), 0). Without it, clipped noise
   remains in the trace.
2. **Per-entry regulariser.** η = 2·median sd(H0), applied once per trace entry. With
   the paper's fixed η = 1e-3, homogeneous noise scores 0.48. With the per-entry η it
   scores 0.002.

**Validation.** On synthetic families the estimator gives:
- static: 0;
- homogeneous noise: 0.002;
- constant heterogeneity: 0.003;
- global synchronous switch: 0.033;
- heterogeneity switched on nested time scales: 0.086.

**Shuffled surrogates.** These are not a valid null for this functional. It rewards
fine-scale variation, which shuffling maximises, so a known multi-scale system scores
below its own shuffle (0.085 versus 0.157). Controls must therefore be matched systems.

## 5. Results
### 5.1 Geb: normalised activity against the null
Five seeds, 20×20 world, 20-neuron limit. Values are normalised median activity,
smoothed over ±50 snapshots (`docs/RESULTS.md`).

| seed | 1M | 2M | 4M | 6M | 8M | null at 8M (or last) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 17 | 27 | - | - | - | 1 |
| 2 | 17 | 36 | 52 | 58 | 89 | -5 |
| 3 | 18 | 23 | 56 | 72 | 106 | -2 |
| 4 | 17 | 30 | 45 | 20 | - | 0 |
| 5 | 19 | 32 | 57 | 69 | 99 | 4 |

Seeds 1 and 4 stagnated, at 2.68M and 6.50M steps (5.3).

**Calibrated test** (`docs/M2.md`)

| horizon | runs | real slopes (per 1e6 steps) | null slopes | max null-window slope | group p | Hedges' g |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2M | 5 | 6.3-23.5 | -1.3-0.0 | 2.5 | 0.031 | 1.70 |
| 4M | 5 | 9.1-14.0 | 0.0-0.4 | 1.0 | 0.031 | 4.45 |
| 8M | 3 | 6.1-7.3 | -0.4-0.3 | 0.4 | 0.125 | 4.67 |

Seed 1 at 4M is the rescued run. Each group p is the minimum attainable for its number
of seeds.

**Findings**
- Every real slope exceeds every null window at every horizon.
- 1 of 15 null tests is flagged at α = 0.05 (the seed-5 null at 8M, p = 0.022). This
  matches the nominal false-positive rate.
- A fixed per-run trend rule, by contrast, classified that same null as 3b.
- The component class matters. With whole rules as components, the real runs are only
  class 2, which agrees with Channon's argument for using predecessor plus link bits.

### 5.2 Geb: scaling (Channon 2019)
Maximum individual complexity, averaged over steps 0.5-1M (`docs/SCALING.md`).

| L | N | runs | complexity (mean ± 95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 20 | 5 | 9.30 ± 0.52 |
| 10 | 40 | 5 | 11.64 ± 0.29 |
| 10 | 80 | 5 | 11.34 ± 0.93 |
| 10 | 160 | 5 | 11.34 ± 0.72 |
| 20 | 20 | 5 | 10.97 ± 0.46 |
| 20 | 40 | 5 | 11.64 ± 1.15 |
| 40 | 20 | 5 | 12.31 ± 0.96 |
| 40 | 80 | 5 | 14.62 ± 0.81 |
| 80 | 160 | 3 | PENDING (interim 16.1-17.0) |

**Joint scaling (N = 2L)**
- Fit: 9.19 + 2.66·log2(L/10), slope SE 0.22, R² 0.92 (15 runs).
- Channon reports 10.17 + 1.87·log2(scale): the same form, with our slope steeper.
- The likeliest cause is the short 1M-step window. This is not established.

**Other directions**
- **Neurons only:** a plateau from N = 40 onward.
- **World only:** no plateau up to L = 40. This is consistent with the softmin model
  (R² 0.73), which predicts saturation only once L is much larger than N.

### 5.3 Geb: stagnation and rescue
**Mechanism.** Frozen populations consist of agents with saturated outputs that spin or
circle in isolation. None ever faces an occupied cell, so no reproduction or kill action
can take effect.

**Rescue.**
- Every run archives a checkpoint every 250k steps.
- A run with no birth for 100k steps exits.
- It is resumed from the newest archive at least 250k steps before its last birth, on a
  new random stream.

**Outcomes.**
- Seed 1 froze at 2.68M. After the rescue it reached 4M and classifies 3b (normalised
  median 72; its null 11).
- One scaling run (L10/N80, seed 15) was rescued once.

Rescued runs are trajectories conditioned on not freezing, and are reported as such.

### 5.4 MSPD on Geb
Each world is replayed for 2048 cycles; window 64, lags 1 and 4. Carriers are cells with
17 states (empty; heading × action).

| world | MSPD | mean heterogeneity h |
|---|---|---|
| unevolved (random genomes, 2000 steps old; 5 seeds) | 0.0010-0.0015 | 0.17-0.21 |
| evolved (4-8M steps; 4 seeds) | 0.026-0.036 | 0.010-0.020 |

- **Separation.** One-sided exact Mann-Whitney test: p = 0.008, the minimum attainable.
- **What it measures.** Unevolved worlds are more heterogeneous but stationary. Evolved
  worlds are less heterogeneous, and that heterogeneity is reorganised over time.
- **What it does not show.** There is no trend across evolutionary time within a run
  (Spearman ρ = 0.40, p = 0.13). The metric correlates with occupancy (ρ = −0.80). At
  this observation scale, MSPD separates evolved from unevolved dynamics but does not
  track open-ended growth.

### 5.5 BFF: emergence, instability, diversity
Twenty seeds at the paper's defaults (`docs/M3.md`).
- **Emergence.** 9 of 20 reach high-order entropy ≥ 1 within 16,384 epochs (45%; Wilson
  95% CI 26-66%). Transitions occur between epochs 2305 and 14,081.
- **Instability.** In 3 of the 9, replicators went extinct within 2048 epochs, and
  entropy returned to the random baseline. The original paper describes "zero-poisoning"
  episodes; here the soups did not recover within the observation window.
- **Diversity.** The dominant replicators differ between origins:
  - they copy either by pulling bytes in (`,`) or by pushing bytes out (`.`);
  - one is bracket-heavy;
  - instructions executed per interaction differ tenfold between them (268-2268).
- **MSPD.** Soups with established replicators score 0.0024-0.0054. Random and collapsed
  soups score 0.0013-0.0018. The separation is complete (p = 0.005, 4 vs 6 soups), with
  the same signature as Geb: lower but temporally organised heterogeneity.

### 5.6 BFF: 2D variant
On the 240×135 grid, about 75% of programs are paired per epoch, so one 2D epoch
provides about 0.19 well-mixed epochs of interaction.
- **Short runs.** 0 of 20 seeds transition within 16,384 epochs, equivalent to about
  3000 well-mixed epochs.
- **Long runs.** 3 of 4 seeds transition within 131,072 epochs, at about 5000-15,000
  well-mixed-equivalent epochs.
- **Localisation.** Replicators remain localised, covering 15-43% of the grid.
- **MSPD.** It separates only the best-covered soup. Patchy systems need a spatially
  resolved estimator.

### 5.7 Coupling BFF heredity to Geb (preliminary)
**Mechanism.** Each agent's genome is 64 bytes. It is decoded by Geb's developmental
system into a neural network, and executed as BFF on [genome | buffer] at reproduction;
the child is the buffer. Founders are the dominant replicators of six transitioned
soups. Shadow nulls breed with the same operator. Prior work and design details are in
`docs/M5.md`.

| variant | rule | result |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | random buffer | Heredity decays from 20-30 to about 6-8 informative bytes, against 1.4 by chance. Activity is class 1 at 2M steps; Geb with built-in heredity is class 3b. |
| v2 | zero buffer | The population collapses to blank genomes, which copy trivially. |
| v3 | v1 plus viability (a child is born only if its code passes on ≥ 16 bytes) | Heredity is pinned at the 16-byte threshold for at least 800k steps. |
| controls | random founders | No heredity and no evolution in every variant. |
| A | v3 with an engineered whole-genome copier | PENDING (64/64 at 20k steps) |

**Reading.** The M3 replicators copy fragments, and Geb's ecology gives weak selection
for transmitting behaviour-encoding bytes. Heredity therefore settles below what
cumulative adaptation needs. Variant A, an engineered high-fidelity copier, tests whether
fidelity is the only barrier.

## 6. Discussion and limitations
**Scale**
- Geb results use 5 seeds at 8M steps. The calibrated group tests are limited by the
  number of seeds, not by effect size.
- The scaling test uses 1M-step runs, shorter than Channon's.

**Rescue and conditioning**
- Rescue conditions trajectories on survival.
- In BFF, transition statistics depend on the observation window, and collapse is
  common.

**MSPD**
- It separates evolving from non-evolving dynamics in two unrelated substrates, but does
  not measure growth within a run.
- It needs matched controls, and it fails in patchy systems.

**Coupling**
- The coupling result is a single seed per variant. It is reported as preliminary.

## 7. Reproducibility
- **Code and data.** All code, tests and result files are in this repository.
- **Tests.** `ctest` covers golden equivalence for Geb and BFF, resume, rescue, MSPD
