# L4: co-evolving challenges (design)

Goal (DESIGN.md L4): pressure toward general problem-solving that comes from a changing environment, not
from scoring agents, so fitness stays endogenous. The owner's constraint from earlier discussion: a fixed
hazard only teaches agents to beat that hazard; the challenges themselves must evolve open-endedly.

Base world: v8 (growing world and neuron limit, kin colonies, colony reproduction, division of labour), the
configuration whose individual complexity kept rising to 5M steps. v9 showed earning space is not what drives
complexity, so L4 does not tie growth to collective births; growth stays on a schedule (or yoked), and the
new pressure comes from challenges.

## Prior work (arXiv check, 2026-11-18)

- POET (1901.01753) and Enhanced POET (2003.08536): environments and agents co-evolve; a child environment is
  kept only if it is neither too easy nor too hard for current agents (a minimal criterion band), and
  Enhanced POET keeps the most novel environments that pass it.
- Minimal criterion coevolution (MCC, the basis of POET): solvers must solve at least one maze; mazes must be
  solved by at least one solver. No fitness ranking on either side.
- Regret-based environment design (ACCEL, 2203.01302; TRACED, 2506.19997): curricula from agent regret. These
  score the learner and need a gradient-trained agent, so they do not fit endogenous fitness; noted, not used.
- Adversarial co-evolution with quality diversity (2505.06617): fixing one side caps open-endedness; both sides
  must evolve. Digital Red Queen (2601.03335): continual adaptation to a changing opponent in Core War.
- Known failure modes of co-evolution (Ficici and Pollack 1998, cited in 2601.03335): cycling and mediocre
  stable states. Countermeasures from POET: an archive of past challenges and novelty among challenges.
- Brain-body-environment co-design survey (2512.04770): POET-style loops as the reference framework.

## Mechanism: evolving locks on the land

Geb agents already act through prefix matching: an action fires when the summed activity of output nodes
whose character starts with that action's bit prefix exceeds a threshold. L4 reuses that machinery.

- **Locks.** Some cells carry a lock with a genome: a bit string (the key) and an effect. An agent can use a
  locked cell (move into it, or give birth into it) only if, at that moment, its outputs present the key:
  the summed activity of its output nodes whose character starts with the key exceeds the threshold, exactly
  as for an action. Nothing is scored; an agent that cannot open a lock simply cannot use that land.
- **What locks guard.** New land from world growth arrives locked, and locked cells are fertile (a birth
  there is cheaper or more likely). Opening locks therefore matters for reproduction, the only fitness.
- **Locks evolve (minimal criterion band).** Each lock counts how many distinct agents opened it over a window.
  A lock opened by at least one agent but by less than a set share of the local population reproduces into a
  neighbouring cell with a mutated key (bit flip, insertion, deletion: keys can lengthen). A lock nobody opens
  for long decays to open land; a lock everyone opens dissolves (it is no longer a challenge). Challenges thus
  track the edge of what agents can currently do, on both sides, without a fitness function.
- **Archive and novelty.** Dissolved locks enter an archive; new land occasionally receives an archived key,
  and among locks that pass the band, those with keys least similar to existing ones reproduce first. This
  counters cycling and the mediocre stable states of plain co-evolution.

## Staged plan

1. **L4a, static key locks.** Locks with fixed random keys, and the minimal criterion band as above. Measures:
   share of land opened over time, key length of the locks still being solved, agent complexity.
2. **L4b, temporal keys.** A key becomes a short sequence of patterns that must be presented over consecutive
   updates. This requires memory in the network and is the first step from pattern matching toward computation.
3. **L4c, cooperative locks.** A lock that opens only when two adjacent agents present two different keys at
   once. This ties L4 to the collectives: coordinated, role-differentiated groups open what individuals cannot.

## Controls

- **No locks** (v8 as is): baseline complexity and diversity.
- **Frozen locks**: locks exist but never evolve, so a solved world stops posing challenges.
- **Random churn**: locks change keys at the same rate as in L4 but at random, not by the minimal criterion.
  This separates adaptive co-evolution from mere environmental change (the Red Queen null).

Primary measures: maximum individual complexity and diversity against these controls (as for v8 and v9), plus
the evolution of the challenges themselves: key length and novelty over time, which should keep growing if the
challenge side is open-ended.

## Implementation sketch (Geb C++ port)

- `World`: a per-cell `Lock` (key bits, opener counts, age), saved in checkpoints; a lock population step at
  cycle boundaries (reproduce, decay, dissolve), with its own counter RNG stream.
- `Organism::try_to_move_forward` and `try_to_reproduce_with_organism_in_front`: before using a locked cell,
  test `action_output(key) > kTA` with the existing prefix-sum code.
- Locality: lock checks touch only the target cell, so the parallel engine's dependency rule is unchanged;
  lock reproduction runs at cycle boundaries.
- Flags: `-locks`, `-lock-band`, `-lock-frozen`, `-lock-churn`; logged per stats interval: locks, mean key
  length, share opened, archive size.

## L4a as built (`-locks`, `-lock-frozen`, `-lock-churn`, `-lock-frac F`)

- collectives bit 128. A lock is a key of 0/1 genes (initial length 4). An agent enters a locked empty
  cell, by moving or by giving birth into it, only if `action_output(key) > kTA`. This is the same prefix
  sum Geb uses for actions; noise outputs are below kTA, so chance never opens a lock. A blocked birth falls
  back to the existing replace rule.
- 25% of cells start locked (`-lock-frac`), and all land added by world growth arrives locked.
- Every 1000 cycles (counter RNG stream keyed by time):
  - a lock opened by 1-8 distinct agents puts a mutated copy of its key (flip, insert, or delete, minimum
    length 2) on a random free neighbouring cell;
  - a lock opened by more than 8 agents dissolves;
  - a lock opened by nobody for 5 windows decays.
- Frozen: locks never change. Churn: each lock is re-keyed at random with probability 1/10 per window, at
  the same key length. This is the same order as the evolving arm's turnover, about 0.2 events per lock
  per window in the smoke run. The match is approximate; the realised rates are logged and compared.
- Archive and novelty selection are not built yet. They are added if the evolving arm shows cycling.
- Requires `-crng`. The checkpoint keeps locks, mode and counters.
- Checks:
  - resume through growth events is bit-identical (seed 2, grow every 4000, split at 10500);
  - 2 threads are bit-identical to 1 over 55k steps, including a growth event;
  - the golden tests are unchanged.
- Log: `time: K<t>K locks N opened N meanlen X maxlen N born N dissolved N decayed N` every 100 steps.
- Smoke run (seed 1, growth every 50k, to 179k): the lock count stays in a band between growth events.
  The mean key length rises from 4 to about 6 between growth events, the maximum reaches 10, and newly
  locked land is mostly decayed, then partly re-colonised by evolved keys.

## Seed 3: the lock population went extinct

- l4a_s3 started at 94 locks. By 12k steps it had none: 56 dissolved (too easy) and 113 decayed (unopened).
- After that, no new lock can be born, because only an existing lock reproduces. Until the first growth at
  500k, which brings new locked land, l4a_s3 is a no-lock world.
- This is the disengagement failure of co-evolution (cited in 2601.03335): the challenge population is lost
  when it drifts out of the learner's reach. The design's archive (re-seeding from past keys) is the fix,
  and stage 1 does not have it.
- Seeds 1 and 2 never went extinct but fell as low as 6-12 locks between growth events, so all three seeds
  are exposed to this.
- For the next version (L4a2): a lock floor, so that when the count drops below a minimum, a key from the
  archive of recently dissolved keys is placed on a random empty cell. l4a_s3 is kept as is; it shows the
  failure.
- l4r_s2 final (500k windows): 9.47, 10.44, 10.68, 11.56, 12.00 (single sample at 3M).

## L4a2: lock floor with archive (`-lock-floor`, resets_v14)

- Dissolved and decayed keys enter an archive, a ring of 256.
- After each lock step, if fewer than size/32 cells are locked, archived keys are placed on random lock-free
  cells, each with one bit inserted. An empty archive gives fresh random keys. The count is logged as
  `refilled` on the K line.
- Checks: the test suite passes, and resume is bit-identical (seed 3, split at 15,500). With the extinction
  seed (3), the count holds at the floor (12 at L20) instead of 0, and `refilled` reaches 54 by 27k.
- Observation: most refilled keys decay unopened, so the population sits at the floor. The floor keeps
  challenges present but does not by itself make them solvable.
- Runs l4b_s1-3 are queued after the seed 3 controls.

## L4b: fertile locks (`-lock-fertile`, resets_v15)

The design (above) says locked land is fertile, but stage 1 built only the barrier, so opening a lock gave
nothing beyond ordinary land. That fits the result that evolving locks recover the cost of locks without
exceeding no locks.
- Now a birth onto a lock the parent opened also places a twin on the next free, unlocked cell forward,
  from the same parents and with the same colony. The count is logged as `twins` on the K line.
- Checks: the test suite passes, and resume is bit-identical (seed 2, growth every 4000, split at
  10,500). The smoke run gave 819 twins in 20k steps.
- Runs: l4c_s1-3 (floor plus fertile), queued after l4n_s4. Comparison: L4a2 (floor only) and the matched
  no-lock runs. If fertile locks exceed no locks, a twin-at-random control (twins not tied to solving a
  key) separates the reward for solving from a plain population boost.

### Design input (arXiv check, 2026-11-20)

- The Emergence of Complex Behavior in Large-Scale Ecological Environments (arXiv 2510.18221, v3 Dec
  2025). With no rewards, some complex behaviours appear only above a threshold of world size and
  population, and larger scales make behaviour more stable.
- Bailey, "Continuously evolving rewards in an open-ended environment" (JMLR 2025). Survival adjusts the
  challenge directly.
- Implication: our ceiling of L52, about 2,700 agents, may be below such a threshold. The null result of
  locks against no locks may be limited by scale rather than by reward design. That points to a scale test
  (L80 and above, with the parallel engine) before further reward tuning, and it is compute-bound on 4 cores.

## Scale test (queued 2026-11-20)

- Motivation: arXiv 2510.18221 reports a world size and population threshold for complex behaviour, and our
  L4 effects are small at L52.
- Runs: l4La_s1-2 (evolving locks) and l4Ln_s1-2 (matched no locks). The world starts at L52/N84 and grows
