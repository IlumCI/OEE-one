# L3: Collectives (Kin Colonies in Geb)

## Aim
Biology repeatedly raised its complexity ceiling through fraternal major transitions, in
which groups of related individuals became units of selection (Maynard Smith and
Szathmáry). L3 adds the minimal ingredients for such a transition to Geb, the substrate
already validated as open-ended (M1-M2).

Related work:
- DISHTINY: Moreno and Ofria, "Toward Open-Ended Fraternal Transitions in
  Individuality", Artificial Life 25(2), 2019, and the life-history follow-up,
  arXiv 2104.10081;
- multilevel selection at the origin of life, arXiv 2608.22348 (2026).

## Rules (`-collectives`; off by default, Geb remains bit-exact)
1. **Bonds.** Two new actions use output prefixes `000` (bond) and `001` (unbond all).
   Both prefixes are unused in Geb.
   - A bond forms with the agent in front, only if both are in the same kin colony and
     within 2 cells of each other.
   - Each agent has at most 4 bonds.
   - Bonds are physical: they break when the partners are more than 2 cells apart, or
     when a partner dies.
2. **Budding.** A newborn joins its parent's colony and bonds to the parent when both of
   these hold:
   - the parent's bond output was active;
   - the parent's group has fewer than 32 members.

   Otherwise the newborn founds a new colony, acting as a propagule.
3. **Groups.** A group is a connected component of live, in-range bonds.
4. **Combat.**
   - No killing within a group.
   - An attack succeeds only if the attacker's group is at least as large as the
     victim's.
5. **Unchanged.** Shadows and nulls are as in Geb; the group rules are ecological, not
   genetic.

## Design history
- **v1: any-to-any bonds at any distance.** The whole population fused into one group
  within 15,000 steps. No kills were possible, the world filled, and births stopped.
- **v2: physical bonds (2-cell range).** Same outcome. In a full world every agent has
  neighbours within range, so bonds percolate across the lattice.
- **v3: kin colonies with a size cap (the current rules).** At 60,000 steps:
  - 80-100 groups;
  - mean group size about 3;
  - largest group 76;
  - 70% of agents bonded;
  - births continuing;
  - population 285.

## v3 result: evolved immortality (l3_s1, l3_s2, 2M steps)
**Group statistics** settled early (by about 300k steps) at:
- mean group size 3.0-3.8;
- 69-77% of agents bonded;
- largest group 75-106.

Random-grouping controls (coin-flip bonding), at matched times of 400-700k steps:
- mean group size 2.1-2.4;
- 62-67% of agents bonded;
- largest group 27-61.

**Activity** (`tools/activity/report.py`): class 3b in both seeds, with normalised median
activity of 1236 and 1196 at 2M steps; nulls flat. Plain Geb has 23-36 at 2M steps.

**Artifact.** The mean agent age is about 600,000 steps in the L3 runs, against 12 in the
controls and 5 in plain Geb.
- Colonies evolved permanent bonding, which made them invulnerable under the combat rule.
- The population became near-immortal and almost static.
- The large activity values therefore reflect persistence of immortal agents, not
  continuing innovation.

**What it does show.** Grouping behaviour did evolve: the controls, whose grouping
cannot evolve, did not freeze. But it evolved as an exploit of the protection rule, not
as a productive transition.

**Cause and fix.** Geb has no natural death, so protection from combat amounts to
immortality.

v4 adds senescence: death after 1000 updates (`-senescence`). Plain Geb agents average
about 5 updates of age, so the cap only binds on protected colonies, which must then
reproduce to persist.

## v4 result: evolved grouping gives unbounded diversity (2M steps)
**Configuration.** v4 adds senescence (death after 1000 updates) to the v3 rules. Each
run has a second-shadow null. Activity uses predecessor + link-bit components.

| run | grouping | mean agent age | mean group size (end) | class | diversity (2nd-half mean) | normalised median | normalised total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| l3v4_s1 | evolved | about 220-290 | 3.6 | 3a (unbounded D) | 92.3 | 51 (null 5) | 13050 |
| l3v4_s2 | evolved | about 210-280 | 3.1 | 3c (unbounded D and median) | 84.2 | 25 (null 2) | 4344 |
| l3v4ctl_s1 | random (coin flip) | 12-15 | 2.3 | 3b (bounded D) | 42.1 | 10 | 2852 |
| l3v4ctl_s2 | random (coin flip) | 10-14 | 1.9 | 3b (bounded D) | 39.6 | 25 | 2261 |

Plain Geb (M1), same configuration without collectives or senescence: class 3b, with
diversity bounded at about 30 at 2M steps.

**Reading**
- With evolvable grouping, diversity becomes unbounded. Seed 2 reaches 3c, the highest
  class in Bedau's taxonomy, which plain Geb never reaches.
- With grouping present but random, the result stays 3b with bounded diversity, as in
  plain Geb. The effect requires heritable, evolving grouping, not grouping per se.

**Correction after the 4M-step confirmation run (seed 3).** Diversity, sampled at

| run | 0.5M | 1M | 1.5M | 2M | 2.5M | 3M | 3.5M | 4M |

Diversity doubles in the first 1M steps, then plateaus at about 100-115 for 3M steps.

Condition comparison (`tools/activity/compare_conditions.py`): 3 evolved runs against 5
controls (3 random-grouping, 2 senescence-only):
- **Diversity level** (mean over the second half of each run): evolved 84-111, controls
  31-42. Exact permutation p = 0.018, the minimum attainable; Hedges' g = 5.8.
- **Diversity growth** (slope over the second half): p = 0.071, not significant. Seed 3's
  slope is −2.9 per 1e6 steps.

The 3a/3c labels for seeds 1 and 2 came from 2M-step runs that ended during the rise
phase.

**Corrected conclusion.** Evolvable kin-colony grouping raises the plateau of sustained
evolutionary diversity by roughly 2.5-3 times over every control. That is a robust
level effect. It does not, on the 4M-step evidence, make diversity unbounded. The new
organisational level adds room for diversification once; it does not yet keep adding
it. Seeds 4-6 are running to 4M steps.

**Interim update with seed 4 (4 evolved vs 6 controls, `docs/results/l3/compare_interim_s4.md`).**
- Seed 4 (4M) mean D, second half: 87.0. Its D slope over 2M-4M is +14.1 per 1e6 steps, i.e. still
  rising at 4M, unlike seed 3 (-2.9). Its control (`l3v4ctl_s4`) is at 32.4, slope -2.7.
- mean D: p = 0.0048 (minimum attainable), Hedges' g = 6.3.
- D slope: p = 0.024, g = 1.55. Significant at this n; the slope difference is driven by
  controls being flat or declining, not by every evolved run growing (seed 3 plateaued).
- Reading: the higher plateau is robust; whether evolved runs keep growing is seed-dependent.
  Seeds 5-6 (running) decide the final 6-vs-6 test.

**Final confirmation (6 evolved vs 6 controls, `docs/results/l3/compare_final.md`).**
Evolved: `l3v4_s1..s6` (s1-s2 to 2M, s3-s6 to 4M). Controls: random grouping `l3v4ctl_s1..s4`
(s3-s4 to 4M), senescence only `gebsen_s1..s2`.

| statistic | evolved (range) | controls (range) | exact permutation p (one-sided) | Hedges' g |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean D, 2nd half | 69.4-111.4 | 30.9-42.1 | 0.0011 (minimum attainable) | 4.24 |
| D slope, 2nd half (per 1e6) | -2.9 to +23.4 | -14.2 to +9.6 | 0.014 | 1.40 |

Activity classes, 4M runs, predecessor + link-bit grouping (`docs/results/l3/v4_4M_activity.md`):
evolved s3 3b, s4 3c, s5 3a, s6 3b; random-grouping controls s3 2, s4 3b. No control reaches 3a/3c.

**Conclusion.**
- Evolvable kin-colony grouping raises the diversity level 2-3x over both controls; every evolved
  run is above every control run (complete separation, 12 runs).
- The diversity slope is also higher (p = 0.014), but this is heterogeneous: s4 and s5 still rise
  at 4M, s3 and s6 are flat. Unbounded diversity is not established at 4M; a raised, partly still
  rising plateau is.
- Status of L3: the new organisational level adds a measurable, reproducible increment of
  open-endedness. It does not by itself produce sustained unbounded growth. Next mechanism is
  needed to keep adding room (group-level reproduction, division of labour, nested levels).

**Superseded conclusion (2 seeds per condition, 2M steps):** Unbounded diversity, and one class 3c
run, appear only with evolvable grouping. None of these produces it:
- grouping with random decisions;
- senescence without groups;
- plain Geb.

The new organisational level (kin colonies whose grouping behaviour evolves) opens room
for continued diversification. This fits the major-transitions picture.

**Limits.**
- Only 2 seeds per condition.
- "Unbounded" is a trend classification over 2M steps.
- Confirmation needs more seeds and longer runs, and the M2 calibrated test applied to
  diversity.

## Runs (`runs6/`)
`l3_s1` and `l3_s2`: seeds 1 and 2, 20×20, 20 neurons, second-shadow null, 2M steps.

Baseline: M1 runs (same configuration without `-collectives`).

## Measures
- **Group structure over evolutionary time:** mean and largest group size, fraction of
  agents bonded, colony persistence. Sustained growth or stabilisation of group-level
  organisation is the transition signature.
- **Activity versus null:** the calibrated test from M2, compared with the baseline.
- **Division of labour within groups:** differences in action profiles between members.
  Planned.

## v5: colony reproduction (group-level reproduction)

Motivation: v4 raises the diversity plateau but does not sustain growth. v5 makes colonies
reproduce as units, following the fraternal-transition design of DISHTINY (Moreno & Ofria,
arXiv 2104.10081: kin groups replicate through single-cell propagules; group-gated
reproduction).

Rules (`Params::collectives` bit 8, flag `-colony-reproduction`, on top of v4):
- A colony (all agents with the same colony id, bonded or not) has at most `kColonyMax` = 32
  members. Every birth from a full colony founds a new colony (single-agent propagule).
- Below the cap: budding as in v4. A non-budded child founds a new colony only if its
  parent's bond group has >= `kPropaguleMin` = 4 members; otherwise it stays in the parent's
  colony, unbonded. New colonies are offspring of grown groups.
- No killing within a colony (bonded or not).
- Logged: number of colonies, largest colony, cumulative propagules (checkpointed).

Design history:
- v5a (no colony cap): one colony took the whole world (~400 agents, 1-3 colonies) within
  4k steps, because kin never kill each other and every non-propagule child stays in the
  colony. Same failure as v1 (single immortal group). Rejected.
- v5b (cap 32): 60k-step check, seed 7: 70-98 colonies coexist, largest group 30-32,
  bonded fraction ~0.65.
- Regression: with bit 8 off, the v5 binary reproduces the v4 binary's log exactly
  (seed 7, 30k steps), apart from the extra fields on the group line.

Runs: `l3v5_s1..s3` (`runs6/`, 4M steps, second-shadow null). Baseline: the six v4 evolved
runs and the v4 controls.

### v5 results (4M steps, 3 seeds; `docs/results/l3/v5_*.md`)

| run | mean D, 2nd half | D slope, 2nd half (per 1e6) | class (pb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| l3v5_s1 | 109.8 | 0.0 | 2 |
| l3v5_s2 | 94.9 | 0.0 | 3b |
| l3v5_s3 | 116.7 | +5.8 | 3b |

- v5 vs v4 (4M runs s3-s6): mean D 107 vs 86, p = 0.086, g = 1.1 (not significant at n = 3 vs 4);
  slope p = 0.80 (v5 flatter, g = -0.54).
- v5 vs controls: mean D p = 0.012 (minimum attainable), g = 9.0; slope p = 0.21.
- Group structure is stationary from ~0.5M steps (about 95 groups, 81 colonies, mean group
  size 3.1, bonded fraction 0.72; `tools/l3/group_series.py`).

Conclusion: colony reproduction raises the diversity level slightly further (~25% over v4,
not significant) and makes it more uniform across seeds, but diversity is flat in the second
half of every run (no class 3a/3c). Group-level reproduction with a single-agent bottleneck
and a fixed size cap does not by itself create new room: colony organisation saturates
early and stops evolving. The next lever must make group organisation itself evolvable
(v6 division of labour: running).

## v6: division of labour (on top of v5)

Rules (`Params::collectives` bit 16, flag `-division-of-labour`, implies v5):
- Role per agent from moving averages (time constant 64 updates) of whether it fires
  reproduce and kill: warrior if kill fires more often, else breeder. Roles are behavioural,
  not assigned; the same genome can express either depending on its sensory context.
- Combat power of a group = its size, doubled if the group has >= 4 members and 25-75%
  warriors. An attack succeeds only if attacker power >= victim power (replaces the v4
  size rule). Mixed-role groups beat equal-size uniform groups.
- Logged: warrior fraction, number of groups with >= 4 members, number of those mixed.

Checks: with bit 16 off the binary reproduces v5 exactly (seed 7, 20k steps, all
non-group log lines). 60k-step check, seed 7: 71-84 colonies, largest group 31-32,
warrior fraction 0.24-0.34; at 15k steps 5 of 8 groups with >= 4 members are mixed.

Background (arXiv): theory of reproductive division of labour in collectives with high
relatedness (1403.7556); collective reproduction in evolving neural flocks lists division
of labour as open (2409.13254). No 2025-2026 digital-evolution implementation found.

Runs: `l3v6_s1..s3` (4M steps).

### v6 results (`docs/results/l3/v6_*.md`)

| run | mean D, 2nd half | D slope, 2nd half (per 1e6) | class (pb) | class (rule) | warrior fraction, last quarter | mixed / groups >= 4, last quarter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| l3v6_s1 | 114.3 | -11.7 | 3b | 3b | 0.45 | 7.4 / 7.9 |
| l3v6_s2 | 119.3 | -11.6 | 3b | 3b | 0.35 | 7.4 / 8.0 |
| l3v6_s3 | 97.8 | +1.2 | 3b | 3b | 0.18 | 3.5 / 7.9 |

- v6 vs v5: mean D 110 vs 107, p = 0.30, g = 0.24; slope p = 0.90. No difference in diversity.
- Median activity is unbounded in all six classifications (both groupings, all seeds); in v5 this
  held for 2 of 6. Division of labour sustains ongoing adaptive activity, not diversity.
- Seed 3 lost most of its division of labour late in the run (warriors 0.41 -> 0.18; mixed large
  groups 7.5 -> 3.5): the mixed-role state is not evolutionarily stable in every lineage.

Conclusion: within a 20x20 world, kin colonies (v4), colony reproduction (v5) and division of
labour (v6) all converge on D ~ 95-120. Each later mechanism changes how the plateau is occupied
(uniformity across seeds, ongoing activity) but not its height. This points to a capacity limit
independent of the group rules, most plausibly population size; the L40 runs test this.

### World-size test (queued/running)
Hypothesis: the v4/v5 diversity plateau (D ~ 90-115 with ~300 agents) is set by world size,
not by the group rules. Test: v5 at world length 40 (~1000-1100 agents), `l3v5L40_s1`;
v6 at length 40, `l3v6L40_s1` (queued after the v6 seeds).
20k-step check, seed 7: ~300 colonies, largest group 32, bonded fraction 0.65.
L80/N160 seed 22 paused at its checkpoint (1.17M) to free the core; the L80 scaling row
already uses the 0.5-1M window.

#### Interim, `l3v5L40_s1` at 1.03M steps
| grouping | D real (2nd half) | D null | class |
|---|---|---|---|
| pb (predecessor + link bits) | 322 | 336 | 3b |
| rule (whole production rule) | 381 | 87 | 2 |

For comparison, v5 at L20 (4M): pb 110 (null 65), rule 117 (null 7).
- Rule-grouped diversity is 3.3x the L20 value with ~3.7x the population (N ~ 1150 vs ~300),
  and 4.4x its own null. Diversity height scales with population, consistent with a capacity
  limit set by world size.
- At L40 the pb-grouped real D does not exceed its null: the pb grouping saturates with drift
  in large populations, so at L40 the rule grouping is the informative measure.
- D is still classified bounded at 1M; growth vs plateau at L40 is decided at 4M.

Replication at ~1.02M steps (rule grouping, real / null): `l3v5L40_s2` 373 / 92, `l3v6L40_s1`

+5% per doubling of time, class 2 (bounded D). At L40 diversity also levels off, at a ceiling
~3.5x higher than at L20.

## v7: growing world (on top of v6)

Motivation: v4-v6 plateau at the same diversity in a 20x20 world, and the L40 interim shows
diversity height roughly proportional to population. If capacity sets the ceiling, a world
whose capacity keeps increasing should let diversity keep increasing. Caveat: this makes
growth possible, it does not force it. The test is whether evolution fills new space with new
kinds of agents (D tracks capacity, real well above null) or with copies of the incumbents.

Mechanism: `World::grow(L)` enlarges the torus at a cycle boundary; agents keep their
coordinates (they occupy one corner block), new cells are empty, so a frontier opens along
the old seam. Driver schedule (absolute, so resume-safe):
L(t) = min(grow_max, L0 + grow_by * floor(t / grow_every)); flags `-grow-every`, `-grow-by`,
`-grow-max`. `Params::world_length` is updated, so checkpoints carry the current size.

Checks:
- Growth off: bit-identical log to the v6 binary (seed 7, 15k steps).
- Growth on (seed 7, L 20 -> 32 by 20k steps): no errors; colonies ~100 -> ~200.
- Resume across growth: run to 10k, checkpoint, resume to 20k = uninterrupted 20k run
  (all 880 log lines identical).

Runs: `l3v7_s1..s2`, v6 rules, L 20 -> 52 (+4 every 500k steps), 4M steps (running; started
by pausing `l3v5L40_s2` and `l3v6L40_s2` at ~1.1M and ~1.0M, after they replicated the 1M level).

Interim (rule grouping, 250k windows; L = 20 until 500k, 24 until 1M, 28 from 1M):

Fixed-L20 baseline (v6, `docs/results/l3/v6_L20_dseries.md`): 100-130 from 1M to 4M. After the

D rises at every growth step (staircase), real D stays 5-8x null, and D per agent stays at

Diversity tracks capacity: the fixed-world plateau was a capacity ceiling, and evolution
fills added capacity with new kinds of agents rather than copies.

Related work (arXiv, checked 2026-11-13): environment size drives complexity of evolved

world during a run to lift a diversity ceiling. Closest open-endedness measures for this
setting: OE scores in VLM-guided ALife (2509.22447); Hash Chemistry growth of complexity
(2607.28219).
